Dealer levels

Call wall vs put wall: how to read dealer levels

Dealer levels are the strikes where options market makers hold the most exposure. They do not predict price. They describe where hedging can speed a move up or slow it down. Here is how to read the three levels most traders watch, and what makes each one stop working.

What a call wall is

The call wall is the strike above the price with the largest call gamma, usually where call open interest is heaviest. When dealers are net long those calls, they tend to sell stock as price rises toward the strike and buy it back as price falls away. That hedging can act like a ceiling, so rallies into the call wall often slow down or stall.

What a put wall is

The put wall is the strike below the price with the largest put gamma. Hedging around it can act like a floor: selling pressure tends to fade as price nears it. If the put wall breaks with size, the same hedging can flip and add to the selling.

The flip zone (switch zone)

The flip zone is the price where total dealer gamma changes from positive to negative. Above it, dealers usually hedge against the move, which tends to calm price and keep ranges tighter. Below it, dealers usually hedge with the move, which tends to make moves bigger and faster. On the A&O desk this is the Switch zone. Some tickers do not have a clear one on a given day, and the desk says so instead of guessing.

How to use the levels

  1. Read the regime first. Above the flip zone, expect more back and forth. Below it, expect trend and bigger swings.
  2. Treat walls as context, not targets. A wall is where a move can slow. It is not a promise that price gets there.
  3. Line them up with the chart. A wall that sits on a prior high, a gap or VWAP carries more weight than a wall on its own.
  4. Check the flow. Fresh prints at a strike can move the wall during the day.
  5. Write the invalidation. Decide in advance which level ends the idea.

What breaks the read

  • Open interest shifts during the session, so a wall can move or shrink.
  • Big news, macro data or earnings can overwhelm dealer hedging.
  • Expiration day removes gamma as contracts expire, so levels can lose their pull into the close.
  • Thin tickers with little open interest give weak, noisy levels.

FAQ

Is the call wall always resistance?

No. It is where hedging can slow a rally. Strong buying, news or a shift in open interest can push straight through it.

What is the difference between positive and negative gamma?

In positive gamma, dealer hedging tends to dampen moves. In negative gamma, it tends to amplify them. The flip zone is the line between the two. Read more in how to read SPY positive vs negative gamma.

Why does a ticker sometimes show no switch zone?

If dealer gamma never changes sign across the strikes near the price, there is no flip level to show. That is information too: the regime is one sided for now.

Should I buy at the put wall?

A&O does not tell you what to buy. The put wall is context. Combine it with the chart, flow and your own plan, and decide what would invalidate the idea before you enter.
