AI research

How to use AI for options trading research in 2026

AI can shorten the distance between a market question and a structured research plan. It can organize data, compare contracts and challenge a thesis. It cannot remove uncertainty, repair stale inputs or decide how much risk is right for you. The useful question is not, “What trade will AI give me?” It is, “What should I verify before I make my own decision?”

AI is a research layer, not a signal

Options research is a matching problem. A ticker alone is not a trade. The same market view can lead to a short-dated call, a defined-risk spread, a LEAPS position, shares—or no position at all—depending on the trader’s time horizon, premium budget, liquidity requirements and maximum acceptable loss.

That is where AI can be useful. It can take a large set of inputs and organize them around a specific question. For example: Which expirations give this thesis enough time? How does the bid-ask spread change the cost of entry? What happens if implied volatility falls? Which event would invalidate the setup?

That is different from asking a chatbot for “the best option to buy.” A model can sound certain while using incomplete, delayed or incorrect information. In a 2024 investor alert, the SEC, FINRA and NASAA warned that AI-generated information may be inaccurate, incomplete, misleading or entirely made up. Source quality and timestamps matter.

USE AI FOR

Structure and comparison

  • Turning a market thesis into a checklist
  • Comparing expirations, strikes and strategy structures
  • Summarizing Greeks, volatility and event risk
  • Finding missing assumptions or conflicting evidence
  • Recording the thesis, invalidation and post-trade review
DO NOT OUTSOURCE

Truth, suitability or risk

  • Confirming whether market data is current
  • Checking the actual option chain at your broker
  • Deciding whether a trade fits your account
  • Choosing position size or acceptable loss
  • Placing, changing or exiting an order

A six-step AI options research workflow

01

Define the thesis before looking at contracts

Write the idea in one sentence: bullish, bearish, neutral or volatility-driven; the expected time window; the catalyst; and the level or condition that would make the idea wrong. If the thesis is vague, a ranked contract list only adds false precision.

02

Verify the data and its timestamp

Ask where each input came from and when it was captured. Underlying price, option quotes, implied volatility, volume, open interest, dealer positioning, news and earnings dates can update on different schedules. Treat any output without a source and timestamp as a hypothesis to verify—not a fact.

03

Filter contracts to your rules

Start with the constraints you control: ticker, strategy, expiration window, premium range, maximum loss and minimum liquidity. Then compare strike location, bid-ask spread, volume, open interest and Greeks. The goal is not to find a universally “best” contract. It is to remove contracts that do not fit the plan.

04

Stress-test price, volatility and time

An option can lose value even when the direction is eventually right. Ask how the position may react if the underlying moves less than expected, takes longer to move, implied volatility falls after an event or the spread widens. Delta, gamma, theta and vega describe sensitivities, not guaranteed outcomes.

05

Compare the structure, including “no trade”

Compare a long option with a defined-risk spread, a longer-dated contract, shares or waiting. High implied volatility may change the trade-off between buying premium and using a spread, but it does not automatically make one structure correct. Include the maximum loss, break-even mechanics, liquidity and management complexity of each alternative.

06

Write the decision before sending an order

Record the thesis, entry condition, invalidation, planned risk, relevant event dates and what evidence would change your mind. Confirm the live contract details at your broker. AI can help assemble the research; you decide whether to trade and place every order yourself.

What to ask an AI research assistant

Good prompts expose assumptions instead of requesting certainty. Start with questions such as:

  • What information is missing from this thesis?
  • Which inputs are live, delayed or calculated, and what are their timestamps?
  • How do these expirations differ in theta exposure and time available for the thesis?
  • How wide is the bid-ask spread as a percentage of the option premium?
  • What scheduled events occur before expiration?
  • What would make this setup invalid rather than merely uncomfortable?
  • Which lower-complexity alternative expresses the same view?
  • What evidence argues against the trade?

The best AI output is often not an answer. It is a cleaner list of what still needs to be checked.

Frequently asked questions

Can AI predict which option will be profitable?

No. AI can organize evidence, compare contracts and model scenarios, but it cannot guarantee an outcome. Options buyers can lose the entire premium paid, and some option-writing strategies can create losses beyond the premium received.

What data should an AI options tool show?

At minimum, look for the underlying price and timestamp, expiration, strike, bid and ask, volume, open interest, implied volatility, relevant Greeks and scheduled events. The tool should make clear which values are observed, calculated or generated.

Should AI choose my strike and expiration?

It can help compare candidates against rules you define. The final choice still depends on your thesis, time horizon, premium budget, liquidity requirements and acceptable loss.

Is AO a trading bot or signal service?

No. AO is an educational research platform. Members choose their tickers, strategies, premium ranges, timelines and risk rules. AO does not place trades.
