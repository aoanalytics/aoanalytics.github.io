How to read it

Three numbers that explain the record.

Reached +30% vs closed green

Most picks went up first.

Many picks reached +30% at some point but closed red: the old exit waited for +30% and allowed −50%, so a gain could turn into a loss. That is why the exit changed on Nov 9.

At the mid vs after costs

Real fills cost money.

The mid is halfway between the bid and the ask. A real trade buys at the ask and sells at the bid. On wide contracts that gap alone can turn a small edge into a loss, so we show both.

The signal log vs the picks

Two different questions.

The signal log asks “did the stock move the way the read said?” The picks ask “did the option trade make money after its exit rules?” A read can be right on direction while the option still loses, which is why the signal log can look better.

What went wrong · what we changed

We change the rules in public, with dates.

Each rule set is published with a fingerprint before it runs, so nobody can quietly rewrite it. Every change below was dated, approved and applied only to new picks.

Oct 24

Rules frozen and published

Version 1 was locked with its fingerprint before the first session. Same day, version 2: the strongest reads with no valid price location now wait.

Nov 2 · problem

Chasing stretched moves

Picks were taken on moves the desk itself had flagged as stretched. Version 3: no pick on a stretched read, and none against a reversal call on the same name in the last 45 minutes.

Nov 5 · problem

Index picks against the day's direction

Version 4: an index pick waits when the day has already reached the other side's first target and not its own.

Nov 5

One read for everyone

Version 5: Omega uses exactly the read members see on the board, with the same first-target guard on every ticker.

Nov 6 · problem

Losers bigger than winners

Winners were capped at +30% while losers ran to −50%, so winning half the time still lost money. Version 6, from Nov 9: stop at −30%, sell half at +30%, let the rest run with +15% locked in, and sell anything red at 3:50 PM.

Nov 8

Every setup measured on its own option

Every signal is now saved with what it looked like and whether its own contract reached +30% before −30%. That data decides the next change, not a hunch.

Questions people ask

Straight answers.

Why do other exit plans look better than the official one?

Because they are measured after the fact on the same trades. The official exit was frozen in advance. Plans that let winners run did better on these trades, which is why the official exit moved to a runner-style plan on Nov 9. We keep measuring every plan beside the official one, so a better one has to prove itself on new trades first.

What is Omega, and why can an account show as “locked”?

Omega is our internal paper-trading agent: it trades the model's picks in paper accounts to test them. Each account has a safety switch: if it falls too far from its peak, it stops opening new trades until we review it. The official picks above keep being recorded either way, because they are the model's picks, not one account's orders.

Is this live money?

No. It is paper trading on live option quotes. We show the mid and the after-costs version so you can see what real fills would have done.
