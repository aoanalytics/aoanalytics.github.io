Strategies

One official record. Every new idea on paper first.

Omega's picks are the record. Each new idea runs beside it as a test lane, on paper, and joins the official rules only when it clears a written bar: 30 closed trades over 15 sessions, a profit factor of 1.2 after costs, and a better result than the official rule. Pick one to see its trades.

How the model is changing

Every version is dated. Its results stay on the page.

When the record shows something is off, the rules change for new trades. Old trades keep the rule they were recorded under, so each version can be judged on its own picks.

    Why the exits are +30% / −50% (Rule 4)

    On Omega’s 64 trades, holding at +30% up / −50% down would have won 59.4% of the time, versus 53.1% as actually traded. Holding to the end wins less often (34.6% of the 26 that have expired), but the winners are huge. From Oct 22, +30% / −50% is Omega’s official exit rule.

    How it is built

    The rules came first.

    Picked by the rules, at the time.

    Each pick is the model's own read at the moment Omega took it. Nothing is picked afterwards, and nothing before Oct 5, 2026 is shown or backfilled.

    The entry is the mid price.

    Entries and exits are the option's mid price at the time, the standard for paper records, before the bid/ask spread and commissions.

    Every trade is kept.

    No result is edited, backfilled or removed. Each trade keeps the rule it was recorded under; corrections are new, dated entries.

    Built on retail data.

    A standard broker data feed and free public sources, sampled every few seconds to a few minutes. Any edge here comes from the model, not from paying for speed.

    Live testing: these agents are being built in the open

    Omega and the signal engine are paper-trading agents under active development. We are traders first, and we change the rules when the record shows us something is off. What we are building toward:

    • One clear exit rule: from Oct 22, Omega takes profit at +30% and stops at −50%, chosen by testing every exit rule on its own trades.
    • Adaptive take profit, stop loss and trailing, sized from the market, in test lanes beside the official rule; one joins only if it beats it.
    • Trading it as a book: per-trade and open-risk limits by account size, a daily profit goal that banks the day, and a daily portfolio review, so results come from a system rather than single bets.
